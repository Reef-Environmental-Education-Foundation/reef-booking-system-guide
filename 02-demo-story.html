The Example

Educator: Jordan Lee  ·  School: Coral Shores Elementary  ·  Program: Ocean Explorers Expedition*  ·  Group: 32 students, 5 chaperones  ·  Goal: a hands-on marine conservation field experience  ·  Outcome: clicks "Ready to Move Forward"

*Staged originally as a 2-day Expedition rather than a 1-day Discovery Program, to reuse REEF's already-tested demo booking rather than build a new one from scratch — see the Honesty Notes in the original Sept 3 Command Center for why. Everything about the story below works identically for a Discovery Program; only the length changes.

Staff-Side Story

Phone / email inquiry arrives

Jordan emails REEF directly: "Hi — I teach 5th grade at Coral Shores Elementary, and I'm looking to bring 32 students and 5 chaperones down for a marine conservation experience this spring. Can you tell me more?"

Today, whoever receives that email either routes it into one of the two Asana intake forms, or manually creates a task using the standard checklist. There's no automatic capture of a raw email — the public intake forms are still draft mockups.

Staff captures it live

A Coordinator creates one record in EPO: Jordan's name, Coral Shores Elementary, an estimated group size of 37, program format interest, and what they're hoping to get out of it. OXP Opportunity Stage is set to Inquiry.

→ Open the real EPO record built for this demo

Staff qualifies it live

A quick call confirms: real dates in the works, real budget authority, a workable group size. The Coordinator judges this worth pursuing. The moment the record is saved with Status = Inquiry, a matching Asana task is created automatically — routed to OXP New Reservation Workflow since this is a program request, not a facility rental. This is Zap 1, live in production.

→ Open the real Asana task built for this demo

Staff creates/links the booking, pricing runs live

Once the Coordinator checks "Ready for REEF Bookings," a priced record is created automatically in REEF Bookings (Zap 2, live): group composition, dates, activities. The formulas take it from there — free chaperones, program fees, supplies, campus facility fee, uplift, total, payment schedule.

Staff generates the proposal system 1 system 2

Today: one click in the "OXP Proposal Generator" Google Sheet builds a branded Google Doc/PDF from the same numbers. Being tested: a webpage-style proposal with the same numbers, a nicer day-by-day layout, and a live "Ready to Move Forward" button — tested against two TEST bookings only, not yet used with a real customer.

Before either proposal goes out — the review gate live, tested end-to-end

The moment a proposal or contract link is generated for a booking, the pipeline automatically creates an Asana review task assigned to Rose, with the proposal and contract links attached. This is a pure process gate — nothing sends automatically when the task is checked off — but no one should hand a link to a customer until that review task exists and has been looked over. It's the main safety net against a half-generated or wrong proposal reaching a real customer.

Staff sees the customer's response, advances the next step tested, not launched

When Jordan clicks "Ready to Move Forward" on the webpage proposal (System 2 only — not live for real customers yet), the system logs the response, the timestamp, and which version of the proposal they saw — and flags the booking for a staff member to review and manually move Status to Verbal Yes. Nothing auto-advances the lifecycle; a person always makes that call.

Customer-Side Story

1. Jordan inquires

Jordan finds REEF online or hears about Ocean Explorers from a colleague, and reaches out describing the group and the goal: get students out of the classroom and into real marine conservation work.

2. Jordan has a discovery call

A REEF Coordinator calls to talk through dates, group size, activities of interest, and budget range — the same information captured in the EPO record above.

3. Jordan receives a proposal

A few days later, a proposal arrives — today, a PDF; in the system being tested, a link to a page. Either way, it lays out the itinerary, what's included, and the price — and either way, a staff member has already reviewed it via the review-gate task before it was sent.

4. Jordan reviews clear price and details

The pricing is presented simply: a per-student rate ($298) and a plain sentence about the free-chaperone policy (4 of 5 chaperones travel free) — not a line-item invoice. Jordan can see exactly what each day looks like.

5. Jordan clicks "Ready to Move Forward"

Convinced, Jordan clicks the button. A confirmation message appears immediately: someone from REEF will follow up on next steps. (If Jordan instead wanted changes, "Request a Change" opens a message box — REEF's own test of this path confirmed both directions log correctly.)

6. Jordan gets next-step expectations

Once the group is confirmed, Jordan's program page goes live — the day-by-day schedule, what to bring, and an "Action Needed" banner whenever something needs their attention (like signing the agreement in Zoho Sign).

The Click-Through Path, in Order

  1. Start: read Jordan's inquiry out loud from this document (or paraphrase it) — this stands in for the original email/call.
  2. EPO record — open it — this is where staff would capture/review the inquiry.
  3. Asana task — open it — the matching to-do, created automatically (Zap 1, live) in the real OXP New Reservation Workflow project.
  4. REEF Bookings record — open it — pricing and status, calculated live, created automatically (Zap 2, live).
  5. Proposal, System 1 — open the Sheet — today's real production tool.
  6. Proposal, System 2 — open the local HTML file (path above) — click through to "Ready to Move Forward" live.
  7. The review-gate task — narrate: the moment either proposal is generated, an Asana review task is created for Rose with the links attached (live, tested end-to-end) — nothing goes to a customer before that task is checked.
  8. Staff sees the response — narrate: a staff member would see the Proposal Response field update on the Bookings record and the Next Action note, then manually advance Status.
  9. Confirmed customer page — open it — what Jordan sees after saying yes.
  10. Status sync back to EPO — narrate: moving the booking to Confirmed automatically pushes status/headcount/price to EPO and comments on the Asana task (Zap 3, live). Completed and Cancelled don't sync yet — that's manual for now.
  11. Where Asana and Scheduling fit next — narrate only: a "Sales Stage" field to mirror Booking Status onto the Asana task doesn't exist yet (blocked on Martha/Rose adding it in Asana); staffing demand should push to REEF Scheduling on Confirmed (designed, not yet built).
